# Placement Process Guide

## Campus Placement Process
Campus placements typically follow this sequence: Pre-Placement Talk (PPT) where the company presents itself, followed by an online test, group discussion or technical assessment, technical interview rounds, and finally an HR interview. Some companies add coding rounds or case studies. Companies visit in tiers: dream companies (highest packages), then regular companies. Most colleges allow students to sit for multiple companies until they get placed. Once placed, some colleges restrict further attempts. Prepare well before the placement season starts, not during it.

## Online Assessment Round
Most companies start with an online test on platforms like HackerRank, Cocubes, AMCAT, or their own portals. The test typically includes: aptitude section (quantitative, logical reasoning, verbal), technical MCQs (CS fundamentals, programming), and coding section (1-3 problems of easy to medium difficulty). Time management is crucial. Attempt all aptitude questions even if unsure, as most tests do not have negative marking. For coding, read all problems first and start with the easiest. Focus on getting correct output for all test cases rather than partially solving multiple problems. Practice on competitive programming platforms regularly.

## Technical Interview Preparation
Technical interviews test your CS fundamentals and problem-solving ability. Core subjects to prepare: Data Structures and Algorithms (most important), Object-Oriented Programming, Database Management Systems, Operating Systems, Computer Networks. For web development roles, also prepare: HTML/CSS, JavaScript, React or Angular, Node.js, REST APIs, SQL. Study your projects thoroughly as interviewers will ask deep questions about your implementation choices, challenges faced, and trade-offs made. Practice explaining your code and thought process aloud. Revise your resume before each interview as questions will come from what you have written.

## HR Interview Preparation
HR interviews assess cultural fit, communication skills, and professionalism. Common topics: introduce yourself, strengths and weaknesses, why this company, career goals, salary expectations, relocation willingness, handling pressure and deadlines. Research the company before the interview: know their products, recent news, company culture, and values. Prepare specific examples for behavioral questions using the STAR method. Dress formally. Be on time. Maintain a positive attitude throughout. Ask thoughtful questions about the role and team. Send a thank-you message after the interview.

## Resume for Placements
Your resume is the first impression. For freshers, keep it to one page. Highlight your strongest projects, internships, and technical skills. Use action verbs and quantify achievements. Tailor your resume for each company or role type. Proofread multiple times. Get feedback from seniors or mentors. Common mistakes: including irrelevant school achievements, listing too many skills without depth, writing generic project descriptions, poor formatting. Keep your GitHub profile active and LinkedIn updated. Include links to deployed projects or code repositories where possible.

## Coding Interview Strategy
Approach coding problems methodically: read the problem carefully, identify the input and output format, think of edge cases, start with a brute force solution, optimise step by step. Practice at least 150-200 problems covering arrays, strings, linked lists, trees, graphs, dynamic programming, and sorting. Focus on patterns rather than memorising solutions. During the interview, think aloud, ask clarifying questions, write clean code, test with examples, and discuss complexity. If stuck, try a different approach or simplify the problem. Time yourself while practicing to build speed.

## Company Research
Before each interview, research the company: what products or services they offer, their technology stack, recent news or funding, company mission and values, Glassdoor reviews for interview experiences. Understanding the company helps you tailor your answers, ask informed questions, and show genuine interest. For startups, understand their product and market. For large companies, know the specific team or division you are interviewing for. Check LinkedIn for people in similar roles to understand career progression.

## Common Mistakes in Placements
Not preparing CS fundamentals thoroughly. Applying to roles without understanding the job description. Not practicing mock interviews. Poor resume formatting. Not having projects to discuss. Lying on resume or in interviews. Not asking questions in the interview. Being overconfident or underconfident. Not following up after interviews. Giving up after initial rejections. Not maintaining a preparation schedule. Cramming the night before instead of consistent preparation. Not sleeping well before the interview day.

## After Getting Placed
Once you receive an offer, review the offer letter carefully: package, role, location, joining date, bond terms, relocation benefits. Negotiate if appropriate, especially for higher packages or preferred locations. Continue learning and building skills before joining. Complete any academic requirements. If you have multiple offers, make a decision based on role quality, learning opportunities, company culture, and long-term growth rather than just the highest package. Inform companies you are declining politely and promptly. Help your peers prepare by sharing your interview experiences.
