# Resume and ATS Preparation

## What is an ATS (Applicant Tracking System)
An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software used by companies (like Workday, Taleo, Greenhouse) to filter, scan, and rank resumes before a human recruiter reviews them. ATS parses the text in your resume and matches it against the job description. If your resume is not ATS-friendly (e.g., uses complex columns, tables, graphics, or unrecognized fonts), the parser might fail to read your text, scrambling your data. Optimising your resume for ATS means using standard formatting, clear section headings, and exact keyword matches from the job description. Over 75% of resumes are rejected by ATS before human eyes ever see them.

## Resume Sections and Order
A strong resume for campus placements and early-career professionals should include these sections in this exact order: 1) Contact Information, 2) Education (since it's your strongest asset right now), 3) Experience (Internships, part-time roles, open-source contributions), 4) Projects (2-3 significant projects with deep technical details), 5) Skills (Programming languages, frameworks, tools). Optional sections include Certifications, Achievements, and Extracurricular Activities. Use standard, simple section headings (e.g., "Experience" not "My Professional Journey") so the ATS knows exactly what data it is looking at.

## Contact Information Dos and Don'ts
DO include: Your full legal name (largest font on the page), professional email address (firstname.lastname@email.com), phone number, city and state (full street address is a security risk and unnecessary), customized LinkedIn profile URL, and GitHub or portfolio URL. 
DON'T include: Your photograph (can cause ATS rejection and bias), date of birth, gender, marital status, nationality, or religious affiliation. Do not label the fields (e.g., don't write "Email: abc@gmail.com", just write "abc@gmail.com"). 

## Writing Effective Bullet Points (XYZ Formula)
Never write paragraphs. Use 3-5 bullet points per role/project. Start every bullet point with a strong action verb (e.g., Built, Developed, Designed, Spearheaded, Optimized, Reduced). Follow Google's XYZ formula: "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]." 
Weak: "Made an API in Node."
Better: "Built a REST API using Node.js and Express."
Best (XYZ): "Built a REST API using Node.js and Express that handled 500 concurrent users, reducing data retrieval latency by 40%." Focus on the impact and the technology used.

## Quantifying Your Impact
Numbers make your resume stand out and prove your claims. Quantify wherever truthfully possible. Instead of "Improved website performance," write "Reduced page load time by 40% (from 3s to 1.8s) by implementing lazy loading and image compression." Even for academic projects, you can quantify: "Collaborated with a team of 4," "Designed a database schema with 12 tables," "Wrote 50+ unit tests achieving 85% code coverage," "Processed a dataset of 50,000+ records." Numbers act as visual anchors for recruiters skimming your resume.

## Skills Section Strategy
Organize your technical skills by category to make them readable. Example categories: Languages (JavaScript, Python, C++, SQL), Frameworks/Libraries (React, Node.js, Express, Next.js), Databases (PostgreSQL, MongoDB), Tools/Platforms (Git, Docker, AWS, Linux). 
Do NOT use progress bars, pie charts, or rating systems (e.g., "Java: 4/5 stars")—ATS cannot read them, and it only highlights what you don't know. Only list skills you can confidently discuss in a technical interview. Soft skills (Leadership, Teamwork) should NOT be listed here; they must be demonstrated through your experience bullet points.

## Project Descriptions
For freshers, projects are your substitute for experience. For each project, include: Project Name, Role (if team project), Dates, and Technologies Used. Write 3-4 bullet points detailing what it does, your specific technical contributions, and measurable outcomes. Focus heavily on technical decisions and architecture: why you chose certain technologies, how you solved key challenges (e.g., state management, database indexing, authentication), and what architecture you used. Example: "Implemented a JWT-based authentication system with refresh token rotation, securing user data against XSS and CSRF attacks."

## Tailoring for Job Descriptions
Tailoring does not mean rewriting your entire resume. It means reading the job description (JD), identifying required skills and keywords, and mirroring those exact keywords naturally in your resume. If the JD asks for "experience with React.js and TypeScript," make sure those exact terms appear in your skills and project descriptions. If you have the skill but called it something else (e.g., you wrote "TS" instead of "TypeScript"), change it to match the JD. ATS algorithms look for exact or close keyword matches. Do not stuff keywords invisibly in white text; ATS systems flag this.

## Common Resume Mistakes
Avoid these instant-rejection mistakes: 1) Spelling or grammatical errors (shows lack of attention to detail). 2) Using a multi-column format (many ATS parse left-to-right, completely scrambling two-column resumes). 3) Including an Objective Statement (outdated; use a Professional Summary only if you have 3+ years of experience). 4) Writing responsibilities instead of achievements (e.g., "Responsible for writing code" vs "Developed 3 core features"). 5) Including "References available upon request" (it wastes valuable space). 6) Saving as a Word doc instead of a standard PDF.

## Resume Length and Formatting
For students, freshers, and professionals with under 5-7 years of experience, your resume MUST be exactly one page. Use a clean, single-column layout with standard, readable fonts (Arial, Calibri, Times New Roman, Helvetica) at 10-12pt size. Use consistent heading styles (e.g., 14-16pt, bold). Keep margins between 0.5 and 1 inch to maximize space without looking cluttered. Use reverse chronological order within each section (most recent experience first). Always save and submit as a PDF (unless explicitly asked for a .docx) to preserve formatting across devices. Name the file professionally: `FirstName_LastName_Resume.pdf`.

## Online Profiles and Portfolios
Your resume is just a summary; your online profiles provide the proof. Maintain consistent information across your resume, LinkedIn, and GitHub. LinkedIn: write a concise headline, a summary highlighting your passion/tech stack, list projects, and actively engage with tech content. GitHub: pin your best 4-6 repositories. Every pinned repo MUST have a detailed README file with project descriptions, tech stack, screenshots/GIFs, and local setup instructions. A green contribution graph helps show consistency. If you have a portfolio website, ensure it is responsive, loads fast, and links to live demos of your projects.
